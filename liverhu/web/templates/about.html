How HUnter measures

  1. Find the scan. In each uploaded study the axial CT series is chosen automatically, preferring a non-contrast series and a slice thickness near {{ defaults.preferred_slice_mm }} mm. Which series was used is shown with every result.
  2. Outline the liver. The AI model TotalSegmentator, trained on real CT scans, outlines the liver and its 8 Couinaud segments. A coarse 6 mm model first finds the liver, then the liver-segment model runs on that region only, so an 8 GB lab PC can run it.
  3. Find the falciform ligament. The ligament runs in the fissure between segments 2/3 (left lateral section) and segment 4. On every slice that boundary is marked (yellow dots in the QA image).
  4. Pick one slice and place the circles ({{ defaults.roi_diameter_mm }} mm diameter by default; 5 mm smaller, down to {{ defaults.min_roi_diameter_mm }} mm, only if that size does not fit). All three liver circles go on the same axial slice, in the middle part of the liver, close to the liver's edge (but at least {{ defaults.edge_margin_mm }} mm inside it) and never overlapping.
    • LL: left lobe, inside segments 2/3, so left of the falciform ligament, at least {{ defaults.ligament_margin_mm }} mm from it.
    • RL1: right lobe, front sector (segments 5/8), at the outer right edge of the liver (the left side of the image).
    • RL2: right lobe, back sector (segments 6/7), near the bottom edge of the image, roughly beneath RL1 (it may sit a little toward the middle, following the liver's shape), with at least {{ defaults.min_circle_gap_mm }} mm of liver between RL1 and RL2.
    • SPL: the spleen, the largest circle that fits in it (anywhere in the spleen, at least {{ defaults.edge_margin_mm }} mm from its edge, avoiding vessels at the hilum, cysts and lesions).
    Each circle stays at least {{ defaults.edge_margin_mm }} mm inside the liver and its segments and avoids vessels, lesions and artefacts. If the three cannot fit on one slice, they are placed on separate slices and the case is flagged.
  5. Liver vs spleen: the results also give liver − spleen (the "liver attenuation index") and liver / spleen, both using the mean of the 3 liver circles.
  6. Measure mean, SD, min, max and median HU of the pixels inside each circle, the circle's area (cm²) and diameter, its slice position (z, mm), and the DICOM image number, so you can find the slice in your own viewer.

Automatic checks

  • ⚠ low / ⚠ high next to a circle's mean HU when it is outside the expected range. The range depends on whether the scan used contrast, which is detected from the aorta (≥ {{ defaults.aorta_contrast_hu|int }} HU = contrast), the DICOM tag or the spleen. Non-contrast liver: {{ defaults.hu_range_liver_noncontrast[0]|int }}–{{ defaults.hu_range_liver_noncontrast[1]|int }} HU (below 40 HU = moderate–severe fatty liver cut-off; above 80 HU ≈ average + 2 SD of 3,357 adults, Boyce et al. 2010). Non-contrast spleen: {{ defaults.hu_range_spleen_noncontrast[0]|int }}–{{ defaults.hu_range_spleen_noncontrast[1]|int }} HU. Contrast-enhanced: liver {{ defaults.hu_range_liver_contrast[0]|int }}–{{ defaults.hu_range_liver_contrast[1]|int }}, spleen {{ defaults.hu_range_spleen_contrast[0]|int }}–{{ defaults.hu_range_spleen_contrast[1]|int }} HU (rough). A low liver value is expected in fatty liver; the mark means "look at this", not "error".
  • Incomplete scan: the liver is cut off at the top, bottom or side of the images, or slices are missing.
  • Quality: ✓ good, ⚠ check (noisy, thick slices, unusual liver volume, very uneven liver, relaxed placement) or ✕ poor (very noisy, not Hounsfield units, circles could not be placed). Poor-quality values may not be reliable.

Check every case

Open the QA image. Each circle must be inside liver tissue, LL left of the yellow ligament line, RL1 above RL2, and none should sit on a vessel. Cases with anything unusual carry an amber flag (for example a contrast-enhanced series, a high SD, or a liver not fully covered by the scan).

Notes for the study

  • HU cut-offs for fatty liver from the literature refer to non-contrast CT. For example, liver ≤ 40 HU, or a 48 HU threshold that was 100% specific for moderate-to-severe steatosis. Contrast-enhanced values are not comparable.
  • Use the same settings (circle size, margins) for every scan in a study. The settings used are saved in each result row.
  • Before relying on the automatic values, compare them with manual measurements on a subset of your scans (e.g. intraclass correlation, Bland-Altman).
